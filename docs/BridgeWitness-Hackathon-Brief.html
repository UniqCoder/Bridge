BridgeWitness

Real-time detection of cross-chain bridge exploits that decides on proof, not on guesses: every release must consume a real, unspent deposit, to the wei.

An independent monitor and enforcement point for cross-chain bridges. It replays bridge feeds, flags mismatched deposit/withdrawal pairs, volume spikes and unusual signer behaviour, and raises a risk-scored alert that names the suspected exploit and carries evidence anyone can re-run in a browser.

01 · Problem statementBridges are the biggest target, and exploits outrun humans

CSB-01 (Chainalysis): cross-chain bridges move billions between blockchains and are the single largest source of crypto hacks. Exploits (fake deposits, signature forgery, liquidity draining) often complete within minutes, faster than manual monitoring can react. Expected: a dashboard over a live or replayed feed that flags volume spikes, unusual signer behaviour and mismatched deposit/withdrawal pairs, and raises a risk-scored alert with the suspected exploit type.

Why it is hard

Bridge attacks differ in mechanism but share one effect: the destination chain releases tokens that the source chain never locked. Three common defences each miss it, and we measured two of them on real data:

DefenceWhy it failsMeasured on the real datasets
Signature / quorum checksStolen keys make valid signaturesRonin: 5 of 9 validator keys were stolen (public reports); the signatures were valid
Volume-spike detectionFires late, or not at all, and false-alarms on busy honest periodsRonin: 0 of 2 releases flagged. Nomad: first flag at +2,986 s, by which time 17 releases and $23.6M (14.8%) had already gone; 3.1% false alarms on normal Nomad traffic
Large-transfer ("whale") alertsFire only after funds movedRonin: both releases exceed $1M, but post-execution response recovers at most ~4.5%

The scale

  • Chainalysis estimated roughly $2B stolen from bridges in 2022 (public estimate; motivation, not our result).
  • Ronin (23 Mar 2022): two withdrawals 115 s apart; ~$624M public estimate ($565.4M at dataset prices).
  • Nomad (1 Aug 2022): 382 releases in 167 transactions by 279 recipients, 9 tokens, $159.6M dataset USD, over ~2.5 hours. Copycats reused only 14 distinct message ids (id 4922 appears 279 times).

Who feels it

Bridge operators and their security teams; exchanges and custodians that rely on bridged assets; compliance, forensics and analytics firms; auditors and insurers. (Intended users from our analysis; not yet validated through interviews.)

02 · SolutionCheck the one thing every exploit has in common

BridgeWitness is the CSB-01 dashboard first. It ingests a bridge transaction feed (a replayed public dataset, a simulated attack replay, or a live local chain), runs three detectors on every release, and raises one risk-scored alert that names the suspected exploit and shows its reasons.

The three signals the brief asks for

SignalIn plain wordsRole
Mismatched deposit/withdrawal pairs
Conservation Ledger		A bridge is a vault with two doors: money may leave the far door only if the same amount, for the same person, came in through the near door. We check that for every release, in exact 256-bit integers.Decides: may trigger a hold
Volume spikes
Velocity detector		A smoke alarm for bursts of releases on one route (5-minute count, 5-minute vs 1-hour rate, volume vs the 24-hour median). Threshold fixed on pre-attack data only.Advises: ranks, never holds
Unusual signer behaviour
Signer forensics		Unknown or too few signers, duplicates; the same signer set co-signing a burst; valid signatures on a release nobody paid for (the Ronin pattern).Rules decide; behaviour advises

The alert

Every alert carries a risk score 0–100 with its arithmetic shown and a suspected exploit type: fake deposit, signature forgery, validator-key compromise, unbacked release, message replay, mismatched pair, liquidity drain (incident level), or an advisory volume/signer anomaly. The score is clamp(base(type) + min(15, 3·log10 USD) + campaign ≤ 5) × evidence completeness: a ranking, not a probability.

Beyond detection: what we add

  • Hold before funds move (pre-execution gate) with scope that widens only as evidence demands.
  • Evidence that anyone can re-run: each verdict ships as a bundle your browser re-verifies.
  • Honesty about blind spots: missing data yields unknown, never a false alarm.

03 · Impact and benefitsWhat changes for the people defending a bridge

Earlier, mechanism-independent detection

The backing check does not care how the attacker got in: stolen keys, a verification bug or a fake deposit all produce a release with no matching deposit. On the Nomad replay it flags the first release (+0 s); the volume detector needed +2,986 s.

Fewer, better alerts

382 Nomad violations become one incident with live USD exposure and a copycat signal, instead of 382 pages. Missing data becomes unknown, not a false accusation. Advisory signals cannot hold funds.

A smaller blast radius

The counterfactual replays Nomad under response policies. At 0 s latency, holding the attacked routes limits loss to 13.2%, adaptive scope to 7.0%, and freezing the whole bridge to 5.8%. At 15 minutes, holding routes alone fails (65.8%), which is why scope must escalate to the shared verifier. We show our narrow policy losing where it should.

Trust you can check

Operators, auditors and regulators do not have to believe our alert. They re-run it: header hash, Merkle proofs, accounting rule and SHA-256, all in the browser. Change one wei and verification fails.

USD is the dataset's historical value, not a live price. They are not funds recovered, and we do not claim "funds saved".

Who benefits, and how (intended)

  • Bridge security teams: an independent second opinion that cannot be silenced by the bridge's own trust set, with a hold they control.
  • Exchanges and custodians: an early, explainable signal on the bridged assets they accept.
  • Analytics, forensics and compliance: evidence bundles that can be handed to a third party and independently reproduced.
  • Auditors and insurers: a standard, checkable record of what was released against what was deposited.

Design principles behind them

  1. Invariants decide; ML advises. Only a deterministic rule violation can trigger containment. The ML model (recall 2.34%, 9 of 384 held-out; ROC AUC 0.982 on its raw score) is advisory only and not used for decisions.
  2. Every verdict carries its evidence.
  3. Blind spots are named: missing data means unknown, never "attack" and never "fine".
  4. Smallest sufficient intervention, recorded with its evidence hash.
  5. No inflated claims. A priority is a ranking; AUC is not accuracy; USD has a stated source.

06 · Feasibility and viabilityBuilt on working parts, with the gaps stated

Technical feasibility: what already works

  • Reference engine (Python): exact accounting, SQLite WAL ledger with idempotent ingest and reorg rebuild, Merkle-Patricia receipt/transaction proofs, finalized contiguous scanner; 55 tests passing.
  • Live demonstration (local): two Ganache chains; a compromised-validator release held before execution; a healthy route settling exactly 25 units; 0 public-chain transactions.
  • Performance (local, measured): p50 0.82 ms, p99 2.6 ms per deposit + release pair (SQLite FULL commit, excludes HTTP/RPC; hardware-specific).
  • Frontend: React, three.js, TypeScript; the in-browser verifier matches Python on all 12 reference bundles; every number comes from a script that fails on drift.
  • Data: public, MIT-licensed XChainWatcher datasets, pinned by SHA-256.

Operational viability

  • Light footprint: consumes finalized blocks and receipts over standard RPC; one writer process; millisecond-scale decisions.
  • Independent of the bridge's trust set: it reads public chain data, so it can be deployed by an operator, a third-party monitor or an analytics firm.
  • Safe by construction: volume and ML signals can rank but never hold; holds are scoped, evidence-linked and need an explicit resolution to lift.

Business viability (proposed, not yet validated)

  • Possible models: monitoring-as-a-service per bridge route; an enterprise integration for analytics and compliance platforms (the evidence bundle is the product); open-source verifier with a paid hosted monitor.
  • Why it could hold: checkable evidence is hard to copy by assertion; an open bundle format creates trust and adoption; the hold feature serves operators' response needs.
  • Not done: customer interviews, pricing or revenue modelling. We make no market-size claims.
Key risks. (1) The backing check needs complete source-chain coverage; with gaps it says unknown by design. (2) Finality is an RPC assumption until a light client anchors it. (3) Each production bridge needs its own adapter; liquidity and intent bridges are out of the current route model. (4) Attackers may adapt after a hold. (5) Public datasets lack signer data, so signer signals are demonstrated on simulated and local-chain data.

Synthetic or preview in the prototype

  • Simulated attack replay: 130 releases (101 background, 19 attack, 10 control); background resampled from real Ronin transfers; validators and signatures fabricated.
  • Signer signals: simulated feed and local chains only; Nomad/Ronin data has no signers.
  • Nomad/Ronin verdicts: computed by our TypeScript port of the rule, not yet by the backend.
  • Observer lag, SLA, incident window, copycat thresholds: placeholder constants.
  • Live bridge results: one reference run on local, unaudited demo chains.
  • USP demos and the 12 attacks: simulated bridge, invented deposits and addresses; the checking rule and the proof mechanics are real. Reorg handling, the early-release rule and the overdue timer are a thin engine layer, labelled on screen.

Not built yet
